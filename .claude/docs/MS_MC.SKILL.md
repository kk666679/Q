# Malaysian Compliance Skill (Enhanced)

## Purpose
Provide authoritative, complete, and actionable information on **Malaysian Standards (MS)** that are identical adoptions of international ISO standards. This skill helps organisations, auditors, and compliance officers understand legal obligations, locate standards, interpret amendments (especially climate action changes), implement management systems, and prepare for certification audits in Malaysia.

## 1. Legal & Institutional Framework

### 1.1 Department of Standards Malaysia (JSM)
- **Legal basis**: *Standards of Malaysia Act 1996 [Act 549]*
- **Role**: National standards body and national accreditation body.
- **Output**: Malaysian Standards (MS) – voluntary but often referenced in regulations or contracts.

### 1.2 Adoption Process
- **Technical Committee** develops/recommends adoption.
- **Industry Standards Committee** endorses.
- **JSM Director General** approves.
- **Publication** on MySOL with “identical to ISO xxxx:yyyy, IDT” (no national deviations).

### 1.3 Key Portals
| Portal | URL | Purpose |
|--------|-----|---------|
| MySOL | https://mysol.jsm.gov.my/ | Search, preview, purchase MS (PDF/hardcopy) |
| ISOlutions | https://isolutions.iso.org/portal/ | International standardisation activities |
| JSM Website | https://www.jsm.gov.my/ | News, committees, publications |

## 3. Climate Action Amendments – Deep Dive

### 3.1 Background
- **ISO London Declaration (2021)**: ISO commits to supporting climate action.
- **IAF/ISO Joint Communiqué (2023)**: All management system standards must include climate change considerations.
- **Amendment 1:2024** published internationally on 2024-02-23.
- **JSM adoption** on 2024-05-30 for MS ISO 9001, 14001, 45001, 27001.

### 3.2 Exact Text Changes (identical across all standards)

**Clause 4.1 (Understanding the organization and its context)**  
*Add the following sentence at the end of the subclause:*  
> “The organization shall determine whether climate change is a relevant issue.”

**Clause 4.2 (Understanding the needs and expectations of interested parties)**  
*Add the following note at the end of the subclause:*  
> “NOTE: Relevant interested parties can have requirements related to climate change.”

### 3.3 Implementation Requirements
- **Effective immediately** – no transition period.
- **Audit expectation**: Auditors must verify that the organisation has documented its determination (yes/no) and, if yes, how climate change affects the management system.
- **Documentation**: Update the context analysis and interested parties analysis. Even a statement “climate change is not a relevant issue” must be justified.

### 3.4 Audit Checklist for Climate Amendment
| Item | Evidence Required |
|------|-------------------|
| Has the organisation determined if climate change is relevant to its context? | Minutes of management review, context analysis document, or risk assessment |
| If relevant, how does climate change affect the management system? | Objectives, risks, opportunities, or operational controls adjusted |
| Have relevant interested parties (regulators, customers, community) raised climate requirements? | List of interested parties and their expectations |
| Is the determination reviewed periodically? | Update frequency defined (e.g., annually or when context changes) |

## 4. Compliance Workflows

### 4.1 For a New Standard Adoption
1. **Identify need** – determine if MS exists for your ISO standard.
2. **Search MySOL** using MS number or ISO number.
3. **Purchase standard** – PDF or hardcopy.
4. **Gap analysis** – compare existing system to MS requirements.
5. **Implementation** – update documentation, processes, training.
6. **Internal audit** – verify compliance.
7. **Certification** – engage accredited certification body (e.g., DSM, SIRIM, TÜV).

### 4.2 For Climate Amendment Integration
```
Step 1: Assign responsibility (e.g., QMR, EMS manager, ISMS lead)
Step 2: Review clause 4.1 and 4.2 of your existing MS
Step 3: Determine relevance of climate change
  - If relevant: Identify specific impacts (e.g., flooding risk to operations, carbon reporting obligations, customer demands)
  - If not relevant: Document justification (e.g., “no physical or transitional climate risks identified”)
Step 4: Update interested parties analysis to include climate requirements (e.g., Bursa Malaysia sustainability reporting, customer net‑zero targets)
Step 5: Integrate into risk register, objectives, and operational controls
Step 6: Train relevant personnel
Step 7: Update internal audit checklist
Step 8: Present evidence during next surveillance audit
```

### 4.3 For HR Standards (MS ISO 10018:2012)
- **Scope**: Guidelines, not requirements. Helps integrate people involvement into ISO 9001 QMS.
- **Key areas**: Competence management, empowerment, communication, recognition, training.
- **Use case**: Organisations wanting to improve employee engagement within their quality management system.

## 7. Examples & Templates

### 7.1 Example: Climate Change Determination Statement (for clause 4.1)
> “Following the amendment to MS ISO 9001:2015, XYZ Sdn Bhd has determined that climate change is **a relevant issue** because:
> - Our supply chain includes regions prone to flooding, affecting raw material availability.
> - Major customers require Scope 2 carbon reduction commitments.
> - Government regulations (e.g., National Climate Change Policy) impose reporting obligations.
> 
> Therefore, we have included climate risk in our risk assessment (ref. QMS‑RISK‑2025‑01) and set an objective to reduce electricity consumption by 15% by 2027.”

### 7.2 Example: Not Relevant Justification
> “ABC Trading has determined that climate change is **not a relevant issue** for its context because:
> - Operations are office‑based with no physical climate exposure.
> - No customer or regulatory requirements related to climate change apply.
> - Business activities have negligible carbon footprint.
> 
> This determination will be reviewed annually or when circumstances change.”

## 8. Common Q&A

**Q: Can I use an ISO standard without an MS equivalent?**  
A: Yes, for internal purposes. But for accredited certification in Malaysia, the certification body will require the MS version if it exists. If no MS exists, they may certify to the ISO directly.

**Q: Are Malaysian Standards mandatory by law?**  
A: Generally voluntary unless referenced in legislation (e.g., certain product standards). Management system standards like MS ISO 9001 are voluntary but may be contractually required.

**Q: How do I know if an amendment is adopted?**  
A: Search MySOL for the base standard plus “Amd 1:2024”. JSM publishes amendments as separate records.

**Q: What is the difference between MS ISO 10018 and ISO 30414?**  
A: MS ISO 10018 is adopted; ISO 30414 (human capital reporting) is not yet adopted as an MS. Use ISO 30414 if you need the international guidelines, but certification to an MS is not possible.

**Q: Where can I find the technical committee members?**  
A: Contact JSM directly or search for “Technical Committee on Quality Management” on the JSM website.
